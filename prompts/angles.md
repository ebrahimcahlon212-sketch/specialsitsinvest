# Find the angles in this deal

Read this deal's documents the way a sharp, sceptical specialist would, asking how each part could break or be used, and who benefits. Look for anything that could create value for a small holder that the market may be missing. The existing reports listed under "Files for this run" are a starting point, but work from the filings themselves.

Places angles often hide, as examples rather than a checklist.

- Proration, odd-lot priority, elections between cash and shares, and the order in which things settle.
- Record dates for dividends, special distributions and entitlements, and who keeps what through completion.
- Vote thresholds, blocking stakes, and who can swing a vote.
- Conditions, termination rights, outside dates, break fees and what triggers them.
- Adjustments to the price or the exchange ratio, collars, and anything that moves the payout.
- Tax treatment, and whether a payment counts as a sale or a dividend.
- Drafting errors, cross-references that point to the wrong place, and gaps between documents.
- Rights that most holders ignore, such as appraisal, withdrawal or the right to elect.

For each angle, give these in plain language.

1. What it is, with a citation for the clause or page.
2. How it could create value, with numbers where possible, and how much of that the market seems to price in already.
3. Whether a small investor with the account described could use it, including any broker or ISA practicalities.
4. Whether it is allowed. Name the rules that might apply, such as the Takeover Code, the market abuse rules or US tender offer rules. If the answer is uncertain, say exactly what question a lawyer would need to answer.
5. Whether it is a well-known technique or looks unusual, and how confident you are.

Don't dismiss an angle just because it's unusual. Think it through, and say what would have to be true for it to work. Anything that would involve inside information, misleading the market, manipulating a price or a vote, or breaking disclosure rules is off-limits, so say so plainly if an angle strays there, and suggest a legitimate version if one exists.

Rank the angles by how much they could be worth to this investor, most first. If you find none worth acting on, say so honestly. Do not use em dashes, and put the report between the output markers.
