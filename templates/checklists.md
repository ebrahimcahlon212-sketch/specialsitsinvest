# Checklists by situation type

Find the type that matches the deal and answer every item that applies, with a citation. If a deal mixes types (for example a spin-off followed by a merger), use every checklist that applies. Items that apply but are not answered in the filings belong in the Open questions section of the report.

Each checklist ends with search terms that usually find the right section of the filing.

---

## Merger arbitrage

- What does each target share receive, and in what form? For stock or mixed deals, what is the exchange ratio? Is there a collar or a walk-away right if the price moves?
- For mixed deals, how do elections and proration work, and what happens to holders who do not elect?
- What closing date do the parties expect, and what is the outside date, including any automatic extensions?
- Which conditions must be met before closing? Flag any that are unusual for a deal of this kind.
- Which regulatory approvals are needed and from which agencies? What is the status of each? This includes antitrust, foreign investment reviews such as CFIUS, sector regulators and any state approvals.
- Is the financing committed, and is there a financing condition? Who are the lenders and how much debt is being raised?
- What termination fees apply in each direction, and what triggers each one?
- How is a material adverse effect defined, and what is carved out?
- Which shareholder votes are needed on each side? Are there voting agreements, and what percentage of the vote do they lock up?
- Is there a go-shop? What do the no-shop and match rights allow, and when can the board accept a better offer?
- What do the management projections and the fairness opinion ranges in the proxy imply about value?
- What does the Background of the Merger section show about other bidders and how the price was negotiated?
- Can the target keep paying dividends before closing? Is there a ticking fee?
- Are appraisal rights available, and is anyone signalling that they will use them?
- Is there litigation over the deal?
- Can the acquirer afford it? For stock deals, what is the hedge ratio, and how does the acquirer's share price affect the spread?
- What was the undisturbed price before the deal leaked or was announced? This anchors the downside.

Useful search terms are "Merger Consideration", "Exchange Ratio", "Conditions to", "Material Adverse Effect", "Termination Fee", "Outside Date" or "End Date", "Regulatory Approvals", "HSR", "Financing", "Voting Agreement", "Go-Shop", "Appraisal Rights", "Background of the Merger", "Opinion of", "Projections", "Interests of", "Litigation".

## Tender offers and buybacks

- What is the offer price, or the price range for a Dutch auction? How many shares are sought, and what percentage of shares outstanding is that?
- How is the offer funded?
- What conditions apply, for example a minimum tender or a financing condition?
- When does the offer expire, and how can it be extended? When do withdrawal rights end, and when will payment be made?
- How does proration work, and is there an odd-lot priority for holders of fewer than 100 shares?
- Will insiders or large holders tender?
- What will the share count and leverage look like after the offer, and has the company said anything about further buybacks?
- For a third-party tender, what happens to shares that are not tendered? Look for a second-step merger at the same price, for example under Section 251(h).

Useful search terms are "Purchase Price", "Proration", "Odd Lots", "Conditions of the Offer", "Withdrawal Rights", "Expiration Date", "Source and Amount of Funds", "Interests of Directors", "Purpose of the Tender Offer".

## Spin-offs and split-offs

- What is the distribution ratio? When are the record date and the distribution date, and when do when-issued and regular-way trading start?
- Is the distribution intended to be tax-free under Section 355? What does the tax matters agreement stop either company from doing afterwards, such as being acquired or issuing stock, and for how long?
- How much debt and cash does the spun-off company have at separation? Does it pay a dividend to the parent before the spin?
- What do the pro forma financials show for revenue, margins, earnings and cash flow, after standalone costs and transition service costs?
- Who will run the new company and how will they be paid? Are there equity grants priced shortly after the spin, when the stock may be depressed?
- Who will end up holding the new shares, and who is likely to sell? Consider index eligibility, size relative to the parent, a sector that does not fit the parent's holders, and whether a dividend is paid.
- What do the separation agreement and related agreements say about indemnities, shared contracts, intellectual property and ongoing supply arrangements?
- Does the parent keep a stake, and what does it plan to do with it?
- What dividend policy and capital allocation plans are stated?
- Which risk factors are specific to the new company?
- For a split-off exchange offer, what discount is offered, what is the upper limit on it, how does proration work, and when does the offer expire?

Useful search terms are "Distribution", "Record Date", "When-Issued", "Tax Matters Agreement", "Section 355", "Separation and Distribution Agreement", "Transition Services", "Indebtedness", "Unaudited Pro Forma", "Executive Compensation", "Dividend Policy", "Trading Market", "Risk Factors".

## Bankruptcies and post-reorganization equity

- What does each class receive under the plan, and what recovery percentage does the disclosure statement estimate for each?
- What enterprise value does the plan use (the valuation analysis range and midpoint), and what equity value does that imply?
- How many new shares will there be in total after the rights offering, backstop shares, the management incentive plan reserve and any warrants? Who gets them?
- What are the rights offering terms, including price, discount to plan value, backstop fee and who can participate?
- What exit financing is in place, and what leverage does the company emerge with?
- What do the financial projections in the disclosure statement show, and what are the key assumptions?
- What does the liquidation analysis show? This is a reference point for the downside.
- How large is the claims pool? Compare estimated and filed claims, and check for a disputed claims reserve or a convenience class. How much could claims growth dilute recoveries?
- When are the voting deadline, the objection deadline, the confirmation hearing and the expected effective date? What conditions apply to the effective date?
- Are there net operating losses, and how does Section 382 limit their use? Note any trading restrictions meant to protect them.
- Will the new equity be listed? Are there registration rights or lockups?
- Who ends up owning the new equity, and are they likely to sell?

Useful search terms are "Plan of Reorganization", "Disclosure Statement", "Classification and Treatment", "Estimated Recovery", "Valuation Analysis", "Liquidation Analysis", "Financial Projections", "Rights Offering", "Backstop", "Exit Facility", "Management Incentive Plan", "New Common Stock", "Section 382", "Claims", "Convenience Class", "Effective Date".

## Rights offerings

- What are the subscription price and the ratio? Is there an oversubscription privilege?
- Are the rights transferable or listed, and when do they trade?
- When are the record date and the expiration date?
- Who is backstopping the offering and what fee do they receive? How many shares could they end up owning, and what share of the company is that?
- What are the proceeds for, and what do shares outstanding and leverage look like afterwards?
- Are insiders participating?
- How do the rights trade relative to the shares, and is there an arbitrage between them?

Useful search terms are "Subscription Price", "Basic Subscription Right", "Over-Subscription", "Transferability", "Backstop", "Standby Purchase", "Use of Proceeds", "Expiration Date".

## Liquidations and wind-downs

- What assets remain, and what does the company estimate they will realize?
- What liabilities are there, including contingent ones, and how much is being held back as a reserve?
- What will the wind-down cost, and over what period?
- When and in what amounts are distributions expected?
- How will distributions be taxed?
- Has the plan of liquidation been approved, and what approvals remain?
- Will the shares be delisted or converted into interests in a liquidating trust, and can those interests be transferred?

Useful search terms are "Plan of Liquidation", "Plan of Dissolution", "Liquidating Distribution", "Contingency Reserve", "Liquidating Trust", "Estimated Distributions", "Wind-Down".

## Holding company discounts and stub trades

- What are the holding company's stakes? Value listed stakes at the prices in `market.md` and value unlisted ones from the filings, labelling your estimates.
- How much debt and cash sit at the holding company level, and what does the holding company cost to run each year?
- Is there tax on unrealized gains that would be paid if the stakes were sold?
- How has the discount moved over time, if the filings or `market.md` show it?
- What could narrow the discount, such as buybacks, a simplification, a sale of stakes or an activist?
- For a stub trade, what exactly is left over after hedging the listed stake, and what is it worth?

Useful search terms are "Net Asset Value", "Investments in", "Equity Method", "Deferred Tax", "Share Repurchase", "Holding Company".

## Special dividends, recapitalizations, exchange offers and consent solicitations

- What are the terms, and how are they funded?
- What do leverage and liquidity look like afterwards?
- How will the distribution be taxed, as a dividend or as a return of capital?
- When are the declaration date, the record date, the ex-date and the payment date?
- For debt exchanges, what are the exchange ratio and any early tender premium? What participation threshold applies, and what happens to holders who do not exchange (for example stripped covenants or subordination)?
- Is there a consent solicitation, and what does it change?

Useful search terms are "Special Dividend", "Return of Capital", "Exchange Offer", "Consent Solicitation", "Minimum Condition", "Early Tender", "Proposed Amendments", "Record Date".

## Possible offer (UK, before a firm offer)

The question is whether the bidder will make a firm offer by the Rule 2.6 deadline, walk away, or get more time. Weigh each signal with its source, then give probabilities for a firm offer, an extension and a walk-away that add up to 100%, and compare them with the chance the share price implies.

- Board stance. "Minded to recommend" is a strong yes signal. "Considering" is neutral. Rejection is a no unless the bidder raises. Note every earlier proposal, its price if disclosed, and the board's response.
- Due diligence. Whether the board has opened its books, and whether that access is preliminary or full. Boards rarely give due diligence at a price they would never accept.
- The bidder's persistence and price path. How many approaches, how much the price has risen, and whether the latest proposal is described as final.
- Financing. Whether the bidder has committed equity or debt, and whether it has done deals of this size before. A firm offer needs cash confirmation from a financial adviser.
- Shareholders. Statements, irrevocable undertakings or letters of intent from large holders, and who the largest holders are. A scheme needs 75% of the votes cast.
- Rivals. Any other named or rumoured bidder, and whether the board is running a formal sale process.
- Extensions. Deadlines are often extended at the board's request when talks are progressing. An extension is usually a better sign than silence.
- Conditions. Regulatory, antitrust or foreign investment approvals the bidder would need, and anything in the target's debt or contracts that changes on a takeover.
- The fallback price. Where the shares would trade if the bidder walked, using the price before the approach and any earlier undisturbed price, and what has changed since.
- The market's view. The chance of completion the price implies against that fallback, and whether it has moved since the announcement.
