# Report template

Use these sections in this order with these headings. Keep each section as short as its content allows. The word counts are limits, not targets. Replace everything in curly brackets.

---

# {Company} ({Ticker}), {situation type}

Prepared {date} from filings dated up to {date of the latest filing used}.

## In plain English

Written for someone new to special situations. At most 250 words plus the table. Explain any term the first time you use it, and cite figures as usual.

Say what is happening in two or three sentences. Then explain how an investor could make money here and how they could lose it, using the actual numbers, for example "the stock is $23.85 and the buyer has agreed to pay $25.15, so if the deal closes you gain $1.30 a share".

Then a short table of the dates that matter, with the columns Date, What happens, What it means for you and Source. Include the last day to act where there is one, such as a tender offer's expiry, an election deadline, a record date or a rights offering's subscription deadline.

End with one sentence on what has to go right for this to work.

## 1. Summary

Five to eight sentences, 150 words at most. Say what the situation is and what you would do (buy, avoid, wait for a better price, or trade it with a hedge). Give the expected return with its timeframe and name the main risk. Say how confident you are. A reader who stops here should know your answer.

## 3. The situation

At most 250 words. Explain what is happening in plain terms. Say who the parties are and what holders receive, and explain the mechanics that affect value, such as elections and proration, collars, CVRs, record dates or distribution ratios.

## 4. Why the opportunity exists

At most 200 words. Who is likely selling or ignoring this, and why. Common reasons include index deletions, spin-off shares landing with holders who do not want them, post-reorganization equity held by creditors who want cash, small size, complexity, and structures that screen badly. Support this with evidence from the filings where you can, for example the shareholder base or how the distribution works. If you cannot find a structural reason for mispricing, say so, because that calls for more skepticism.

## 5. Valuation

Base case value per share, with each key assumption cited. Use the method that fits the situation.

- Mergers and tender offers use the deal consideration, adjusted for timing and the chance of completion.
- Spin-offs use standalone earnings or cash flow against peer multiples. Multiples come from `market.md` or are labelled "Not from the filings".
- Reorganizations use the plan's enterprise value and the recovery waterfall, after all dilution.
- Liquidations and holding companies use asset values net of liabilities and taxes. Deduct wind-down costs or ongoing holding company costs as well.

Add a small sensitivity table for the one or two assumptions that move value the most.

## 6. Catalysts and timeline

A table with the columns Date, Event, Confirmed or estimated, Source, What could delay it. Include filings, votes, regulatory decisions, court hearings, record and payment dates and the outside date, whichever apply.

## 7. Downside case and risks

State the value if the thesis fails and how you arrived at it (for example the undisturbed price or a standalone value), with its probability.

Then list the specific risks, each tied to language in the documents with a citation. Examples are closing conditions, the material adverse effect definition and its carve-outs, financing, regulatory approvals, litigation, tax and dilution. Order them by expected cost, meaning how much each would cost multiplied by how likely it is.

## 8. Document details that change the economics

Terms a quick reader would miss, each with a citation and one sentence on why it matters. Depending on the situation these can include collars and walk-away rights, go-shop and match rights, ticking fees, appraisal rights, voting agreements, tax matters agreements that restrict a spin-off, debt pushed onto a spun-off company, backstop fees and rights offering discounts, claims pools that dilute recoveries, and management incentive plans. List only what is present and material.

## 9. Insiders and incentives

At most 200 words. How management and the board are paid under the new structure, equity grants tied to the deal or spin-off, change-of-control payments, and any insider buying or selling the filings show. Say what these suggest about how insiders expect things to go.

## 10. Open questions

What the filings do not answer and how the investor could find out (a later filing, the court docket, investor relations, or a named data source). Most important first.

## 11. Position and sizing

Filled in at the sizing step using the IBKR account. Until then this section reads "Pending sizing step."

## 12. Where the models disagreed

Final report only. A table with the columns Point, Draft said, Reviewer said, What the filing shows, Decision, Check by hand (yes or no). Include every point a reviewer raised against the draft, including the ones you rejected, and cite the page that settled each one.

## 13. Sources

Each document used, with its date and what it was used for.
