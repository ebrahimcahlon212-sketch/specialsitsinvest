# COMPANY (TICKER), business fundamentals

## In plain English

Two or three short paragraphs for a reader with no finance training. Say what the company does and how it makes money. Then say what it looks worth on its own compared with today's price and any offer.

## 1. What the business does

Its products or services, its customers and end markets, and where it operates. Say what a customer is actually paying for.

## 2. How it makes money

The revenue model written as an equation where possible, for example fleet size × utilisation × day rate. Show which drivers moved revenue over the last few years, with numbers and citations.

## 3. Unit economics

What one unit of the business earns and costs, such as one rental asset, one site, one contract or one customer. Give the payback period and the return on the capital it ties up. Say when a figure is your estimate.

## 4. Financial history

A table of the last three to five years with a source for each figure. Revenue, gross margin, EBITDA, operating profit, capex (split into maintenance and growth if the filings do), free cash flow and net debt. Show statutory and adjusted figures side by side where they differ, and explain the adjustments. Then say what the table shows.

## 5. Cash conversion and capital intensity

How much of profit turns into cash, and how much capital growth needs, measured by the return on capital employed.

## 6. Balance sheet and financing

Net debt and leverage, debt maturities and covenants, leases, pension deficits, earn-outs and anything else a buyer would have to take on.

## 7. Competitive position

Who it competes with and what, if anything, protects its margins from being eroded. Protection can come from scale, switching costs, specialist assets or customer relationships, and the filings should show evidence of it rather than claims.

## 8. Cycle and risks

How exposed it is to its industry's cycle, customer concentration, key people, regulation and operational problems. Rank them by how much each could change the value.

## 9. Management, ownership and incentives

Who runs it, how they are paid, who owns the shares, and what that means for any offer.

## 10. Standalone value

The value of the business on its own, built from first principles. State the assumptions for a bear case, a base case and a bull case, covering growth, margins, capex and the discount rate, and give the valuation calculator's results rather than your own arithmetic. Compare them with the undisturbed share price and with any offer.

## 11. What this means for the situation

Whether any offer is cheap or generous against standalone value, and how likely a higher or competing offer is. How far the shares could fall if a bidder walks away, and why.

## 12. Open questions

What would change the view, and where to find the answer.

## 13. Where the models disagreed

Filled in by the final step. A table with the columns Point, Draft said, Reviewer said, What the filing shows and Decision.

## 14. Sources

Every document used, with its date.
